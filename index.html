About me

I am a postdoctoral researcher at Grand Valley State University, where my research focuses on software engineering and artificial intelligence. I completed my PhD in Computing at the University of Michigan–Flint.

My research aims to make software changes easier to understand, more effective, and safer to accept. I combine empirical software engineering, machine learning, program analysis, and optimization to develop techniques for improving software quality, reliability, and performance.

My current research explores how we can better understand software changes, establish trust in AI-generated code, automate software repair and verification, and guide intelligent software optimization. I am particularly interested in how evidence from software development and evolution can support more reliable automated decisions.

Alongside my research, I have experience teaching undergraduate computer science courses, mentoring undergraduate and graduate researchers, and developing research proposals in collaboration with academic partners.

Research Interests

  • Software Change Analysis

    Understanding the intent, quality, and impact of software changes through fine-grained analysis and mining software repositories.

  • Trustworthy AI for Software Engineering

    Developing evidence-driven techniques for assessing the correctness, quality, and security of AI-generated software.

  • Automated Repair and Verification

    Combining AI, static program analysis, and execution feedback to detect, diagnose, and repair software defects.

  • Intelligent Software Optimization

    Using search-based, learning-guided, and agentic techniques to improve software quality and performance.
